## Mathematical and numerical checks

The [scoring and consistency contract](../SCORING.md) states the target, likelihood, priors and sufficient conditions for posterior concentration. It distinguishes identifiable predictions from correlated coefficient decompositions, fixed-dimensional regularity from growing-feature/adaptive-design questions, and theoretical consistency from finite-sample calibration.

The implementation uses one unweighted Bernoulli observation per current video/channel and fixed proper full-support Gaussian priors. Revisions replace observations. No effective-observation cap, forgetting window or permanent blend restricts the learned correction. Each offset is also a predictor, so its prior influence can be cancelled rather than remaining an irreducible bias. Holdout reservations are finite and release feedback after recording independent evaluation.

A deterministic binomial-count recovery check starts with deliberately wrong prior means `[2, 2, 2]`, standard deviation 0.4 and true coefficients `[-0.4, 1.2, -0.7]` on four fully covered feature contexts:

| Effective observations | Parameter error, L2 | Posterior covariance trace |
| ---: | ---: | ---: |
| 20 | 2.635383 | 0.360106 |
| 200 | 0.559435 | 0.072670 |
| 4,000 | 0.034724 | 0.004721 |

Separate tests show that a wrong context-dependent offset is cancelled, a long sequence of negative overall labels can overcome its prior without a score floor, and absent feature coverage retains uncertainty. One-dimensional posterior moments/predictive probabilities are compared with numerical integration. Dense and matrix-free solves agree numerically, including predictive variance; a 401-feature example is fitted without dropping the dictionary tail. Evidence-sensitivity bounds do not inject persistent noise into the point prediction. These checks support implementation correctness; they do not prove consistency for arbitrary users or substitute for real labels.

- Feedback features are captured outside the short write transaction. A change during fitting triggers a source-identity retry; concurrent writes preserve all latest labels. ## Human validation still required

Open the local dashboard's **Learning and calibration** panel and rate the two categories separately. Scores are hidden within that panel to reduce anchoring; this is not a claim of fully blinded evaluation, because some videos may have appeared in earlier lists. Do not invent negative ratings to obtain class balance.

`learn evaluate` reports held-out Brier score/log loss, prior comparisons, bootstrap intervals and reliability bins. The gate requires thirty paired videos, enough usable training and held-out labels, both platforms and class variation. A detectably worse Brier result fails it. The selected set may not supply enough negative examples; another unseen cohort may then be needed. A wide interval is not proof of equivalence, and curated self-selected feedback cannot establish unbiased platform-wide performance.

After evaluation is frozen, heldouts join training. The stored evaluation identifies the model actually tested; it is not relabeled as an independent test of the subsequently refitted model. Editing its labels, changing the profile or changing the model version invalidates that evidence. Phase 5 is not started automatically.
